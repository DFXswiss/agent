# CRY-02 – Python log verification: membership and platform log, rights, wraps, event rules

Decisions taken where `04-crypto.md` sections 4.3, 4.7, 4.8, 5.10, 5.11, 6.1-6.4 and 8 were silent or ambiguous.

1. **Vector section location.** The log vectors are generated by `agent_hub/log/vectors.py` (`SECTIONS`, discovered by
   the generator of CRY-01) instead of `contracts/tools/vectors_log.py`, so the generator code is under the 100 %
   coverage gate and `mypy --strict` (CRY-01 decision 1). Files: `log.json`, `log-rotation.json`, `log-wraps.json`,
   `log-events.json`, `platform-log.json`.
2. **One code per rule.** L1/L2 (and a changed `org`) `log_chain`; L3 `log_genesis`; L4 `log_signature` (unknown
   author or wrong/malformed signature); L5 `log_rights` unless a dedicated code exists (`last_admin`, `invite_*`);
   L6 `log_epoch` (also `wg_create.epoch != 1`); L7 `invite_consumed`; L8 `log_rights` (the key of a removed author
   is known, so L4 passes and L8 refuses). Accept failures that are not about the invitation's state
   (`by != subject`, kind, subject still active, `sig`, `inv_sig`) are `log_accept`. The first entry of a hub append
   with a wrong `seq` is `seq_conflict`; in a full verification it is `log_chain`. Entry formats follow CRY-01:
   `header_invalid`, `non_ascii`, and `log_type` for an unknown type.
3. **Platform log codes.** P1-P4 use the L codes; a `root` entry whose key differs from the pinned key is
   `platform_root_mismatch` (the client-side code of section 4.8); `device_certify` with bad enrollment signatures is
   `log_accept` (the analogue of `accept`), with a missing/used invitation `invite_not_in_log`/`invite_consumed`.
4. **Archived workgroups** stay readable (keys, R, wraps) but are not writable: no events (`wg_write` false), no
   `grant`, `invite`, `device_assign` or second `wg_archive`. A `rotate` of an archived workgroup is allowed (R after a
   removal may contain it); its right is "admin or explicit write access".
5. **Leaving (`remove` with `subject == by`)** cannot carry the rotation: the leaver is removed by the entry and L8
   forbids its `rotate` entries, and keys created by the leaver would be known to it. Its R is recorded as
   `OrgState.pending` and the batch is accepted without rotations; any principal with the rotate right clears a
   pending scope with a later `rotate` (the hub adds `pending` to `org.rotation_pending`). Decision of this package;
   the specification only covers decertification this way.
6. **Batch rule.** R of a batch is the union of `R(before, after, subject)` over its entries (the decertification
   batch "`device_unassign` for every workgroup + R" needs the union). With R non-empty the batch contains exactly one
   `rotate` per element of R, no other `rotate`, each after the last entry contributing to R. Every epoch the batch
   creates (`genesis`, `wg_create`, `rotate`) and that is current at its end needs a wrap for every entitled recipient
   in the same batch; a missing wrap is `rotation_required` (section 4.7 "all new wraps in one atomic batch",
   `wg_create` "MUST contain ... the wraps of WK_1 for all recipients"). R is computed from the membership log only;
   decertified devices are neither in R nor recipients (`excluded`).
7. **Entitlement and holders (W1/W2).** A recipient is entitled to a scope epoch `1..current` if it can read the scope
   *now* (older epochs are wrapped "on request", section 5.10; removed principals never). A holder of an epoch is its
   creator, any principal that could read the scope while the epoch was current (from the log history, so a wrap
   stays valid after its wrapper is removed), or a principal entitled now. The `cred` readers of a user are the owner
   and every device assigned to a workgroup in which the owner has access (read or write), over all orgs given; the
   CK epoch comes from outside the log (`current_epoch`).
8. **Platform devices** get their keys from the `device_certify` entry at `platform_seq`; an active platform device
   keeps its `platform_seq` (another value is `log_rights`; a re-certified device is removed and assigned again).
   Validity is **per certification** (`PlatformState.valid(seq)`): a device that is decertified and certified again
   with new keys does not make the old certification, nor the old keys the org learnt from it, valid again (review
   finding). Decertification does not invalidate history: log replay checks only that `platform_seq` names a
   certification of the device. In hub append mode (`now`) `device_assign` additionally needs a still valid
   certification and platform access. `PlatformState.excluded(state)` (principals whose certification has ended) is
   passed as `excluded` to readers, wraps, catch-up, batches and the hub's event checks; a reader showing the history
   of a device decertified later may leave it out (the hub refused its events from the decertification on).
9. **Platform access** (ADR-026): the hub answers `platform_access_denied` (not a log code) from its
   `org.platform_access` flag before running the rules; the rules take the flag as `platform_access` (fail-closed,
   default false) and refuse a `device_assign` with `platform_seq` in append mode with `log_rights` as backstop.
   `PlatformState.platform_access(org, private)` gives the flag from the logs (private orgs, else the latest
   `org_platform_access`). Readers do not check it (a later revocation leaves existing assignments in place).
10. **Invitation expiry** is checked only at hub receipt (`now`), expired when `now >= expires`; a reader replaying
    the log does not check it (it cannot know the receipt time).
11. **No-op entries are refused** (`log_rights`): a `role` that does not change the role, a `grant` with the current
    access, an `ungrant` without an explicit grant, a second `device_assign` of the same pair. Deterministic logs are
    easier to compare across implementations.
12. **Default workgroup**: a user invitation never names it (every member writes it anyway, ADR-024 addendum); a
    device invitation may (assignment). `grant`/`ungrant`/`wg_archive` on it are refused.
13. **Rejoin**: a removed principal may accept a new invitation of the same kind; it starts with the rights of the new
    invitation (`remove` deleted all its access and assignments). A former user cannot rejoin as a device or vice
    versa (`log_accept`).
14. **Event check order.** The author must be an active principal at `lh` before E3, because E3 needs the author kind;
    an unknown or removed author is `author_not_writer`. Header field rules of the matrix (`empty`, `set`,
    `scope_id`, `target` = a workgroup of the org at `lh`, `author`, `device`/`entry`/`operation` = UUIDv7,
    `read_target` = non-empty) are `header_invalid`. A future epoch at the hub is `epoch_stale`. The grace period is
    inclusive (`now - rotated_at <= 600 s`); `rotated_at` is the receipt time of the entry that created the current
    epoch (None for epoch 1). E7 is optional for readers (`session_device` when known) and mandatory at the hub (an
    unknown session is `author_not_writer`). E9 compares canonical JSON of the stored and the incoming event.
15. **Kind matrix** is a Python table (`events.KIND_RULES`) compared with `contracts/enums/event-kinds.json` by the
    named test CON-03; the package does not read `contracts/` at runtime.
16. **Catch-up** plans only the current epoch of every scope; older epochs are wrapped on request.
17. **History.** `VerifiedLog` keeps the state after every entry (needed for E5 "at `lh`" and for holders); memory is
    O(entries × principals), fine for clients. The hub uses its projections (`02-data-model.md` section 2.2, HUB-05)
    and these functions for the append of one batch.
18. **Contract defect fixed** (`contracts/changes/CRY-02.md`): `device_certify` now carries `agent_version`, `os`,
    `arch` and `capacity` from the enrollment body, so that browsers can rebuild the enrollment text and check
    `enroll_sig`/`inv_sig` as section 4.8 requires; `GET /v1/platform/enrollments` returns `capacity`.
19. **Re-certification**: a decertified device ID may be certified again with a new device invitation; a device that
    is currently certified cannot be certified a second time (`log_rights`).
20. **CRY-01 generator tests.** Two tests of `tests/platform/hub/crypto/test_vectors.py` assumed that no area section
    exists (`discover()` equal to the crypto sections; `main` on a temporary directory without the real areas). With
    `agent_hub.log.vectors` they had to be adapted minimally: the first compares the prefix, the second replaces
    `discover` by the patched crypto sections. The generator itself is unchanged.
21. **Review findings** (independent review, all high/medium fixed): per-certification validity (8); a rejoining
    user must bring its earlier identity keys (`log_accept`; a device may re-enroll with new keys); the wrapper key
    is taken from an org in which the wrapper is active; at the hub (`check_batch`, `require_active=True`) removed
    principals store no wraps; devices write no events into archived workgroups (4). Kept as specified: an admin may
    rotate a workgroup without access (section 4.7 table "admin or wg writer"); it then knows the new key – the
    specification accepts this, the UI should let writers rotate. The expiry of platform device invitations is
    checked by the hub at enrollment (`POST /v1/device/enroll`), not by `device_certify` (certification comes later
    by the operator). Decisions 11 and 12 are deliberately stricter than the specification; CRY-03 and WCR-02 copy
    them through the vectors.
